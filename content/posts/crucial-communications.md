A couple of years ago, my intern supervisor assigned a project but didn’t give many details. I analyzed it to try to figure out what my supervisor wanted and expected. I asked questions; however, he didn’t give much information.





This project description didn’t denote that I needed to retrieve data from a database. The contract only provided the information about utilizing Python and SQL. I chose to use Python MySQL and researched SQL queries. Tutorials I read explained that I may need to select and retrieve  items from the company database. 





I sent my ideas for the steps necessary to complete the assignment to my supervisor several times, requesting his input. The first and second versions of my ideas didn’t contain writing to a .csv file and retrieving data. I had incorrectly used the word “collect” instead of “retrieve”. After I spent time researching SQL queries while coding, I realized that my supervisor wanted me to print data to .csv file using a select query.   





I added additional details to my steps to complete the project to include the information concerning opening a file, writing to a file, retrieving data, and then closing a file. Finally, my supervisor stated that this was what he wanted.





Because my supervisor didn’t give many details for completing the assigned project, I had the opportunity to practice communicating effectively with him. He also taught me to implement a responsible role in researching and owning the project.
